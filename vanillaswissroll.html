Time is of the Essence

If you've ever taken a general chemistry course, you'd know that there is quite a lot of math involved. Math is how we prove concepts in chemistry. That is why I was very excited when I came across this equation: L^2=4Dt

Karl Pearson, a great statician introduced the idea of the random walk. He described a drunkard's walks as: if a person flips a coin, and it comes up heads the person steps to the left. If it comes up tails, they step to the right. What he didn't know is that this question had been answered twice in the preceding years.

Einstein had been studying milk. In milk, the fat globules of milk move around randomly. Random movement of small particles is called Brownian motion. He reasoned that this motion is because the fat globules are mumping into each other. The motion would be very similar to the random walk.

Louis Bachelier was interested in the movement of stock prices. He created a model for how stock prices move with time, assuming they do so randomly. Unfortunately for him, the movement of stock prices is much more complicated.

In the end, all three men came up with the same mathematical description: L^2=4Dt

D is the diffusion coefficient in cm^2/sec, and it varies based on different materials, l is the distance that the diffusion front has moved in cm, and t is time in seconds

Most foods have similar diffusion coefficients, notably they are all close to water which has D=0.0014cm2 per sec, meaning you can simply use D=0.0014 in your calculations.

There is a mathematical law that tells us exactly how long to cook our food!

The recipe I used says to cook your cookie for 10 minutes. Let us plug this in for time and use it to calculate the crumb of our cookie (the crumb being the solid part). The calculated crumb should take up the entire height of the cookie, we don't want our cookie to be liquid on the inside.

L=Sqrt(4*0.0014(cm^2/sec)*600sec)

L=1.833cm

This means that the crumb of our cookie could extend up to 1.833 cm. I imagine that our cookie should be 1cm-2cm high. Likely, this means that the cookie is cooked all the way through from all directions. Probably not a very gooey cookie.

I will leave you to experiment using this formula, to create your idea of a perfect cookie. Do you want it cooked rock solid (I know I do) or will you leave it gooey on the inside?

Ingredients

Chocolate Sponge Cake

  • 3/4 cup cake flour or all-purpose flour
  • 1/4 cup unsweetened cocoa powder, plus extra for rolling
  • 1 teaspoon baking powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 4 large eggs, separated
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar, divided
  • 1/4 cup vegetable oil
  • 1/4 cup buttermilk or whole milk
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 1 teaspoon espresso powder (optional)

Vanilla Whipped Cream Filling

  • 1 cup cold heavy cream
  • 3 tablespoons granulated or confectioners’ sugar
  • 1 teaspoon vanilla extract
  • 2 tablespoons marshmallow creme (optional)

Chocolate Ganache Topping

  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 4 oz semi-sweet chocolate, finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon light corn syrup (optional)

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F (177°C). Grease and line a 12×17-inch baking sheet with parchment paper. lightly grease the parchment.
  2. Sift together the flour, cocoa powder, baking powder, and salt. Set aside.
  3. Beat the egg whites with 1/4 cup sugar until medium peaks form.
  4. In a separate bowl, beat the egg yolks with the remaining sugar, then mix in the oil, milk, vanilla, and optional espresso powder. Fold in the whipped egg whites in two additions.
  5. Gently fold the dry ingredients into the batter until just combined. Don't overmix.
  6. Spread the batter evenly into the prepared pan and bake until the surface springs back lightly when touched.
  7. While warm, invert the cake onto a cocoa-dusted sheet of parchmentpaper or a clean towel. Remove the backing parchment and carefully roll the cake up. Allow it to cool completely while rolled.
  8. Prepare the filling by whipping the cream, sugar, and vanilla until medium-to-stiff peaks form. Mix in the marshmallow creme if using.
  9. Unroll the cooled cake, spread the whipped cream evenly over the surface, then re-roll the cake without the parchment or towel.
  10. Make the ganache by pouring hot cream over the chopped chocolate (and corn syrup, if desired). Let stand briefly, then stir until smooth and glossy.
  11. Pour the ganache over the filled cake roll, coating the top evenly. Chill until set before slicing.
  12. Store covered in the refrigerator for up to 3 days.

Notes

  • The rolled cake can be prepared a day ahead before filling.
  • The filled cake freezes well before adding the ganache.
  • Either cake flour or all-purpose flour may be used.
  • Espresso powder enhances chocolate flavor but is optional.
  • A 10×15-inch pan can be used, resulting in a thicker cake and longer bake time.
