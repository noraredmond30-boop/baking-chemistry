Shortbread Cookies Recipe

Jump to recipe

The History of Shortbread Cookies

Shortbread cookies originated in Scotland and have become one of the most beloved butter cookies in the world. Traditional shortbread is known for its crumbly texture, rich buttery flavor, and simple ingredient list. Unlike many cookie recipes, shortbread contains no eggs or chemical leaveners, allowing the butter flavor to shine.

These cookies are famous for their melt-in-your-mouth texture. Made from only a handful of ingredients, shortbread relies on careful mixing and proper chilling to create its signature crisp yet tender bite.

Because the dough is easy to prepare ahead of time, shortbread is an excellent make-ahead dessert. The dough can be chilled or frozen until ready to bake, making it convenient for holidays, parties, and special occasions.

While classic shortbread is delicious on its own, many bakers customize it with nuts, spices, citrus zest, or chocolate. Its versatile flavor makes it the perfect companion to coffee, tea, and hot chocolate.

Despite its simplicity, shortbread demonstrates how a few quality ingredients can create an elegant and satisfying dessert. Its buttery richness and delicate texture have made it a favorite for centuries.

Recipe

Ingredients

  • 10 tablespoons unsalted butter, room temperature
  • 1/2 cup confectioners' sugar
  • 1/2 teaspoon pure vanilla extract
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 teaspoon kosher salt (optional)

Directions

  1. Beat the butter and vanilla extract together until smooth and creamy.
  2. Add the confectioners' sugar and salt and mix until combined.
  3. Scrape down the bowl and gradually add the flour while mixing on low speed.
  4. Continue mixing until the dough comes together.
  5. Shape the dough into a rectangular block.
  6. Wrap the dough tightly in plastic wrap.
  7. Refrigerate for at least 1 hour or until firm.
  8. Preheat the oven to 350°F (177°C).
  9. Remove the chilled dough and cut into 1/2-inch thick slices.
  10. Place the slices on a parchment-lined baking sheet about 1 inch apart.
  11. Use a fork or skewer to create a decorative pattern on top.
  12. Bake for approximately 10 minutes, rotating the baking sheet halfway through baking.
  13. Remove when the bottoms are just beginning to color lightly.
  14. Transfer cookies to a wire rack and allow them to cool completely.

The Science of Shortbread

Shortbread gets its distinctive texture from its high butter content. The term "short" refers to how fat coats flour particles and limits gluten development. Less gluten means a more tender, crumbly cookie rather than a chewy one.

Why Chilling the Dough Matters

Chilling solidifies the butter before baking. This helps the cookies hold their shape in the oven and prevents excessive spreading. It also allows the flour to fully hydrate, creating a more uniform texture.

Because shortbread contains no leavening agents, most of its texture comes from the balance of butter, flour, and sugar. Proper chilling ensures the cookies remain crisp, delicate, and beautifully shaped after baking.
