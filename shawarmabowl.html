Why We Cook

Ever wondered why we cook? Maybe you had a busy day, and you were too tired to cook, so you washed and sliced up all the vegetables in your fridge, added it to a bowl, and ate it. If so, you probably ended up with part of the answer.

Cooking food makes it taste better. Let me explain.

There are 2 main groups of flavor molecules; aroma molecules and taste molecules. There are 5 taste molecules: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami. These are sensed by receptors on the tongue, whereas aroma molecules float to the back of the nose, when we chew food. There are near infinitely many aroma molecules. Aroma molecules have to be very small to float on air. Because of this we might say that they are volatile, meaning they evaporate readily.

If you've ever tried to hold your nose when eating a food, you really don't like, you might have noticed that it isn't completely effective. A famous experiment blindfolded people and had them hold their notes while eating pieces of apple onion and potato. The tasters are told to guess which of the three foods they are eating. The foods tend to taste similar until they are about to be swallowed. Then, aroma receptors sense the food passing through, you get the final identity confirmation. If the taster has a cold, the receptors in both the note and mouth are blocked, causing all food to taste bland

Fats, proteins are carbohydrates are very large in comparison to taste and flavor molecules. This means that they are unable to bind to taste receptors on the tongue and produce very little flavor. However, cooking can break down these macromolecules into flavor molecules.Because fats, proteins, and carbohydrates are so large, they provide texture to food.

Shawarma Bowl Recipe

Ingredients

For the chicken

  • 1lb chicken thighs
  • 1 TBSP yogurt
  • 1/2 a lemon
  • 1 tbsp cumin
  • 1tbsp tumeric powder
  • 1TBSP ground coriander
  • 1 TBSP paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/2 tsp cayanne pepper
  • pinch of salt
  • 2 TBSPS oil
  • 1 Bell pepper sliced

For the greek salad

  • 1 cup diced tomatoes
  • 1 cup diced cucumbers
  • Thinly sliced red onion, washed and coated in lemon juice and sumac.
  • 1/4 cup feta, diced
  • 2 TBSPS olive oil
  • 1 tsp basil

For the Rice

  • 1 cup white basmati rice
  • 1/2 TBSP cumin
  • 1/2 TBSP coriander
  • 1/2 TBSP tumeric
  • 1 tsp paprika(optional)
  • 2 TBSPS oil
  • pinch nutmeg(optional)
  • 5 cups chicken stock

Directions

  1. For the Rice: Bring 2 cups of chicken broth to a boil in a medium saucepan. Wash and dry 1 cup of white basmati rice. In a medium pan heat 1 TBSP oil. Add in spices, saute for 10-30 seconds and then add in rice. Cook for 10-15 minutes, and then add to your boiling broth. Simmer the rice and broth mixture for 30-45 minutes
  2. Preheat the oven to 425 degrees
  3. Combine the chicken thighs, oil, bell peppers, yogurt, lemon, and spices in a bowl and mix thoroughly
  4. Place chicken mixture on a tray and bake for about 45 minutes, or until thoroughly browned
  5. thinly slice one yellow onion. season, add 1 tbsp oil and put on a tray.
  6. Add the onion tray into the oven. cook for about 20 minutes
  7. Thinly slice one red onion, wash it to remove the onion flavor and add lemon juice and sumac.
  8. Add tomatoes, cucumber, feta, olive oil, and basil to the red onion mixture.
  9. For the assembly: Add the chicken, onion, and salad to the center of lavash bread in a column. Add yogurt, romaine lettuce, pepperonici, and toum souce if desired.roll up the lavash bread and cook for abou 1-5 minutes on each side. Slice in half and serce.
