Why You'll Love These Cookies

Chocolate chip oatmeal cookies combine the chewy texture of rolled oats with the rich sweetness of chocolate chips. The oats add a hearty texture while the brown sugar helps create a soft and flavorful cookie.

Chilling the dough before baking helps prevent the cookies from spreading too much in the oven. This creates a thicker cookie with soft centers and slightly crisp edges.

A small amount of molasses adds extra depth of flavor and pairs perfectly with the cinnamon, oats, and chocolate. The result is a bakery-style cookie that stays soft for days.

Recipe

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups (188g) all-purpose flour
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon (optional)
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • 3/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup (226g) unsalted butter, softened
  • 1 cup (200g) packed brown sugar
  • 1/2 cup (100g) granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs, room temperature
  • 1 tablespoon molasses
  • 2 teaspoons vanilla extract
  • 3 cups (255g) old-fashioned rolled oats
  • 1 3/4 cups (315g) semi-sweet chocolate chips
  • Flaky sea salt for topping (optional)

Directions

  1. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, cinnamon, baking soda, and salt.
  2. In a large bowl, beat the butter, brown sugar, and granulated sugar until light and creamy.
  3. Add the eggs, molasses, and vanilla extract. Beat until fully combined.
  4. Add the dry ingredients to the wet ingredients and mix until combined.
  5. Stir in the rolled oats and chocolate chips. The dough will be thick and sticky.
  6. Cover and refrigerate the dough for at least 45 minutes.
  7. Preheat the oven to 350°F (177°C). Line baking sheets with parchment paper.
  8. Scoop about 2 tablespoons of dough per cookie and place them 3 inches apart on the baking sheets.
  9. Bake for 13 to 14 minutes, or until the edges are lightly browned.
  10. Let the cookies cool on the baking sheet for 5 minutes.
  11. Transfer to a wire rack and cool completely before serving.

Cookie Baking Tip

Chilling cookie dough improves both texture and flavor. During chilling, the flour absorbs moisture and the fats solidify, resulting in thicker cookies with better flavor development.

however if you prefer thinner cookies, this recipe is still excellent if you choose not to chill the dough.

The Role of Oats

Rolled oats provide structure, chewiness, and a slightly nutty flavor. Unlike quick oats, old-fashioned oats hold their shape during baking and create the classic oatmeal cookie texture.

The oats also balance the sweetness of the chocolate chips, making these cookies rich without being overly sweet.

Baking soda vs. baking powder

Baking powder is approximately 1 part baking soda and 2 parts cream of tartar. Cream of tartar is made up of an acid called potassium bitartrate. it is a byproduct of winebaking. Because baking soda contains baking powder it is easy to substitute one for the other. Therefore, in the recipe above, you may substitute 1 tsp baking soda with 1 tbsp baking powder. This may result in thinner cookies, because the bakings soda within the baking poweder has more acid to react with.
