Why We Cook

Ever wondered why we cook? Maybe you had a busy day, and you were too tired to cook, so you washed and sliced up all the vegetables in your fridge, added it to a bowl, and ate it. If so, you probably ended up with part of the answer.

Cooking food makes it taste better. Let me explain.

There are 2 main groups of flavor molecules; aroma molecules and taste molecules. There are 5 taste molecules: sweet, sour, salty, bitter, and umami. These are sensed by receptors on the tongue, whereas aroma molecules float to the back of the nose, when we chew food. There are near infinitely many aroma molecules. Aroma molecules have to be very small to float on air. Because of this we might say that they are volatile, meaning they evaporate readily.

If you've ever tried to hold your nose when eating a food, you really don't like, you might have noticed that it isn't completely effective. A famous experiment blindfolded people and had them hold their notes while eating pieces of apple onion and potato. The tasters are told to guess which of the three foods they are eating. The foods tend to taste similar until they are about to be swallowed. Then, aroma receptors sense the food passing through, you get the final identity confirmation. If the taster has a cold, the receptors in both the note and mouth are blocked, causing all food to taste bland

Fats, proteins are carbohydrates are very large in comparison to taste and flavor molecules. This means that they are unable to bind to taste receptors on the tongue and produce very little flavor. However, cooking can break down these macromolecules into flavor molecules.Because fats, proteins, and carbohydrates are so large, they provide texture to food.

Cream puffs Recipe

Ingredients

  • 2 cups heavy cream
  • 1 cup milk
  • 2 packages instant vanilla pudding mix
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/2 cup butter
  • 1 cup all purpose flour
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 4 large eggs
  • item

Directions

    For the filling

  1. combine cream, milk, and pudding mix in a bowl. cover the bowl with plastic wrap and place in the fridge to set.

    2. For the pastry

  2. Preheat oven to 425 degrees
  3. Bring water and butter to a rolling boil in a large pot at medium high
  4. stir in flour and salt until the mixture forms a ball. you may want to lower hear.
  5. run the bottom edges of the pot under cold water to cool it down. Be careful to avoid getting water in your dough mixture. beat in eggs, one at a time using a wooden spoon or stand mixer
  6. spoon dough onto an ungreased baking sheet about 2 inches apart
  7. bake in the preheated oven until puffs are golden brown and centers are dry 20 to 25 minutes
  8. Allow puffs to cool. then fill with cream, custard or fruit. Enjoy!

How to make custard filling.

  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1 vanilla bean
  • 1/2 cup granulated sugar
  • 3 tbsps cornstarch
  • 1/8 tsp salt
  • 4 egg yolks
  • 1 ounce of unsalted butter. 2 tbsps cut into cubes
  1. Bring milk and vanilla bean to a simmer. remove from heat, cover, and let steep for 30 minutes
  2. Make an ice bath. set aside
  3. in a heatproof mixing bowl, stir together sugar, cornstarch and salt. whisk in egg yorks until mixture is pale yellow smooth, and fluffy. about 1 min
  4. slowly pour in milk in a thin stream
  5. return the mixture to the saucepan
  6. cook over medium heat, about 5 minutes
  7. once mixture begins to bubble, continue whisking for 1 minute.
  8. off heat, whisk in butter
  9. put mixture through a sieve
  10. refrigerate 2 hrs or cool using an ice bath for 30 minutes and then refrigerate
